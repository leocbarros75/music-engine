# Washed — Brass Auto: first-reading review

Read at quarter note = 139 with even eighths. Let the designated solo line lead; accompany at the lower printed dynamic. Check the transposed key signatures before starting: C-sharp major for B-flat trumpets, F-sharp major for F horns; B major for the low brass. Full-score chord symbols are concert pitch.

## Places to listen closely

| Passage | Review question | Practical adjustment to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Pickup–8 | Does the light Trumpet 1 melody remain clear above the opening chorale? | Reduce accompaniment first; keep the source melody and rhythm intact. |
| 9–28 | Is Horn 1's extended lead passage comfortable and audible? | Agree additional breaths or redistribute a short response to Horn 2 after checking register and transposition. |
| 29–36 | Does the trumpet theme return sound like a change of color, without a large volume jump? | Match the preceding phrase's sound before increasing projection. |
| 37–52 | Can Trombone 1 phrase its lead and turnarounds cleanly at tempo? | Mark alternate slide positions and light legato tonguing where needed. |
| 53–62, both repeat routes | Are Trumpet 2's theme and the horn cue distinct? Is the lower second-ending cue secure? | Rebalance horn accompaniment and check Trumpet 2's lowest written notes. |
| 65–104 | Do the two-bar solo exchanges connect? Are tuba eighths and trombone backbeats buoyant? | Rehearse handoffs in pairs; shorten accompaniment releases consistently. |
| 105–112, repeat | Does Horn 2's vamp lead emerge over the rhythmic texture? | Keep pulse parts light; judge fatigue on the actual repeated route. |
| 113–114 | Does Trombone 1's descending cue, including sounding F-sharp 2, speak cleanly? | Discuss attachment/position choices with the player. |
| 115–123 | Are the four successive lead colors connected through the ritardando? | Agree cueing, final breaths, fermata length, and release. |
| Tuba throughout | Is the F-sharp 1–E 2 foundation clear and rhythmically light? | Balance attacks and air support without making every low note an accent. |

Horn 1 has the greatest nominal sounding share (about 70%). Trumpets have more substantial rests (about 22% and 27% sounding). These are timing measurements, not endurance predictions. An eighth rest at this tempo lasts only about 0.22 seconds; players may need longer, explicitly agreed breaths.

Distinguish a tie (sustain the same pitch) from a phrase slur (connected moving notes). Trombone slurs are musical instructions: the player chooses slide positions and the tonguing needed to avoid unintended glissandi. Any revision to pitch, timing, repeats, or transposition should be reflected in both score and part, then revalidated.
