## Rehearsal priorities

1. Play the pickup and opening with Alto 1 alone; add saxophone support and piano/bass. Keep the 46 source melody segments intact and let the breath releases settle without stretching the tempo.
2. Rehearse bar 9 slowly. B/D-sharp begins on beat 1, E6/9 on beat 2, and F-sharp add4 on the “and” of 4. Bar 10's G-sharp m7 arrives on the “and” of 1. The bass attacks must make these positions clear.
3. Listen to Tenor 1 at 13–28 and Trombone 1 at 37–52. Reduce accompaniment whenever the foreground is obscured. Later melodic endings include editorial rests for breathing; do not lengthen support into those spaces automatically.
4. Rehearse 29–36 with rhythm section and saxophones before adding brass. Walking bass remains even despite the straight-eighth drum backbeat. Piano and guitar should maintain short, distinct attacks rather than both sustaining every chord.
5. Rehearse the bridge from 65 in four-bar groups. Lead switches between Alto 1 and Tenor 1, then between Alto 1 and Trumpet 1 later. Match saxophone staccato length and brass releases. The shout marking at 81 concerns rhythmic lift as well as volume.
6. Isolate both endings at 53–62 and 105–114. Count the repeat route together. Check approaches to the first and second endings at performance tempo.
7. At 115, lighten the rhythm section as the foreground alternates every two bars. Agree on the ritardando at 122 and the final fermata/release at 123; drums let the final cymbal ring.
